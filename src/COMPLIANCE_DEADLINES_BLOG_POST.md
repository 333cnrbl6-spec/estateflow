### DEADLINE #1: GAS SAFETY CERTIFICATE (12-Month Deadline)

**What It Is:**  
Annual gas safety inspection by a Gas Safe registered engineer. Required for any rental property with gas appliances (boiler, cooker, fire, etc.).

**When It's Due:**  
Every 12 months from the date of the last inspection.

**Why It Gets Missed:**
- "I'll book it in January" (then you forget, it's July)
- Tenants don't remind you (they assume it's done)
- You change letting agents, and the new one doesn't inherit the reminder

**Penalty for Non-Compliance:**
- **Fine:** Up to £6,000 per property
- **Prosecution:** Possible jail time (if injury occurs)
- **Liability:** If a tenant is injured due to unserviced gas, you're liable for personal injury claims (unlimited)
- **Eviction block:** You can't evict a tenant for rent arrears if you're non-compliant

**How It Actually Happens:**
> "Sarah managed 8 properties. Her letting agent didn't flag that one property's cert was due. Six months passed. A tenant got minor burns from a faulty boiler. Sarah faced a £4,000 fine *and* a personal injury claim of £15,000."

**How to Never Miss It:**
- [ ] Set calendar reminder for 11 months after last inspection
- [ ] Use a compliance tracker (spreadsheet or property management software)
- [ ] Automate: Use a system that sends you an alert 30 days before expiry

### DEADLINE #2: Deposit Protection (30-Day Rule)

**What It Is:**  
Any tenancy deposit must be protected in a government-backed scheme (DPS, MyDeposits, TDS) and prescribed information must be provided to the tenant.

**When It's Due:**  
Within 30 days of receiving the deposit.

**Why It Gets Missed:**
- You think your letting agent handles it (but they didn't confirm)
- You protected the deposit but forgot to serve prescribed information
- You changed schemes and forgot to re-protect

**Penalty for Non-Compliance:**
- **Fine:** 1–3x the deposit amount (court case almost always won by tenant)
- **Legal cost:** £500–£2,000 (solicitor fees)
- **Example:** Deposit £1,500 → Tenant wins £4,500 claim + your legal costs

**How It Actually Happens:**
> "Mike received £2,000 deposit on January 15. Life got busy. He protected it on February 28—28 days late. Tenant discovered this, claimed in court, and Mike paid £6,000 (3x the deposit) + £1,000 legal fees."

**How to Never Miss It:**
- [ ] Create a system: Deposit received → Protect within 5 days (buffer of 25 days)
- [ ] Document: Keep proof of protection + prescribed info delivery
- [ ] Automate: Send prescribed info template immediately upon deposit receipt

### DEADLINE #3: Right to Rent Checks (Before Tenancy Starts)

**What It Is:**  
From 1 June 2022, all landlords must check a tenant's right to rent in the UK before a tenancy begins. Failure = criminal offense.

**When It's Due:**  
Before the tenancy starts (not after).

**Why It Gets Missed:**
- Confusion about who does it (you or your letting agent)
- Assumption that references cover this (they don't)
- You check a tenant's passport, assume you're compliant (you need to follow the official process)

**Penalty for Non-Compliance:**
- **Fine:** Up to £20,000
- **Criminal record:** Possible
- **Tenant ejection:** The tenant can be reported and deported

**How It Actually Happens:**
> "A letting agent asked a tenant for their passport. The agent thought this satisfied the check. When the Home Office audited the file, they found no official right-to-rent verification. £15,000 fine issued."

**How to Never Miss It:**
- [ ] Use the official UK government right-to-rent checking system (gov.uk)
- [ ] Document: Keep proof of the check on file for 6 years
- [ ] Automate: Use a letting agent or compliance software that handles this before tenant move-in

### DEADLINE #4: Annual Accounts & Confirmation Statement (Companies House)

**What It Is:**  
If you operate through a limited company, you must file:
- Annual accounts (within 9 months of year-end)
- Confirmation statement (annual, within 14 days of anniversary)

**When It's Due:**  
Accounts: 9 months after year-end  
Confirmation statement: Every 12 months (can be done online in <10 min)

**Why It Gets Missed:**
- You assumed your accountant filed it (but they're waiting on your info)
- You use a letting agent and forget you still have company filings
- You forgot the company even existed (if you set one up as a "future" vehicle)

**Penalty for Non-Compliance:**
- **Fine:** £150–£1,500 per month of lateness
- **Director penalty:** Accumulates, even if you're no longer active
- **Strike-off petition:** If 3+ years late, Companies House can dissolve your company (nightmare to reverse)

**How It Actually Happens:**
> "Property investment firm forgot to file confirmation statement for 2 years. By year 3, Companies House struck them off. Directors faced £30,000+ in back fines + legal costs to reinstate the company."

**How to Never Miss It:**
- [ ] Set calendar reminder 6 months before year-end (accounts due)
- [ ] Set reminder 11 months after last confirmation (annual filing)
- [ ] Use accounting software (Xero, FreeAgent) that flags these dates
- [ ] Automate: Hire a Companies House filing service (£50–100/year, worth it)

### DEADLINE #5: Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) Renewal (10-Year Validity)

**What It Is:**  
Before renting a property, you must have an EPC showing its energy rating (A–G). Required when property is first rented or when tenancy begins.

**When It's Due:**  
Before tenancy starts (not retroactively). Expires after 10 years.

**Why It Gets Missed:**
- "I have an EPC from 2016, it's still valid" (actually, it expired in 2026)
- Letting agent said they'd handle it (but they ordered it for the wrong property)
- Confusion about when "first renting" actually is

**Penalty for Non-Compliance:**
- **Fine:** Up to £5,000 per property
- **Rental invalid:** Cannot legally let without EPC
- **Listing removal:** Can't advertise on Rightmove, Zoopla until you have one

**How It Actually Happens:**
> "A landlord listed a 3-bed on Rightmove in April 2026. The old EPC (from 2016) was 10 years old. Rightmove flagged it as expired. Listings were pulled. Landlord had to pay £150 for new EPC (1-week wait). Lost 2 weeks of viewings."

**How to Never Miss It:**
- [ ] Check EPC expiry date when tenancy starts (not 9 months before)
- [ ] Renew 30 days before expiry (to avoid gaps)
- [ ] Track in spreadsheet: Property | EPC date | Expiry date | Next renewal date
- [ ] Automate: Use property management software with EPC renewal alerts

### Step 2: Set Reminders 30 Days Early
**Every deadline should have a reminder 30 days before it's due.**

Why 30 days?
- Gas cert: Time to book engineer (often 2–3 week wait)
- EPC: Time to arrange surveyor
- Companies House: Time to gather docs
- Deposit: Time to process before tenant move-in

### Step 3: Delegate (Don't Dump)
**If you use a letting agent or accountant:**
- Confirm in writing: "You're responsible for X, Y, Z deadlines"
- Check-in quarterly: Ask them to show you their compliance calendar
- Don't assume—verify
